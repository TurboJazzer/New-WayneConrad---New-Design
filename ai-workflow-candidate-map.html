AI Workflow Candidate Map

By Wayne Conrad  ·  October 17, 2026  ·  10 minutes, runs in your browser

Short answer: list up to five manual workflows, score each on four things, and automate first the one with the most hours that is already clear enough to build. A workflow with unclear steps or unwritten approval limits gets mapped before it gets built.

This is for owners of service businesses with 5 to 50 people. It takes about ten minutes. It runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

How the four scores work

  • Hours a week. How long the workflow takes across the whole team. Your own estimate, not a benchmark. More hours means a bigger payback.
  • Steps. Are they written down and the same every time? A workflow can only be built from steps that are agreed.
  • Data. Where the inputs live. One or two business systems can be read and written directly. Paper and memory have to be captured first.
  • Approval limits. Are the limits on discounts, exceptions and sign-off written down? The workflow then sends a person only what is above them.

What happens next

A workflow marked Ready to build or Nearly ready is a candidate for a single AI workflow sprint. Several of them across sales, delivery and finance point to the end-to-end AI ops engine. If automations already exist but some break or overlap, the AI cleanup sprint comes first. A workflow marked Needs groundwork or Map it first starts with a written map of how it really runs.
