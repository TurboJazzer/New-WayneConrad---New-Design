Scenario 1 of 4 · Enquiries

A 12-person agency where leads sit and discounts wait on the founder

Single AI workflow sprint · enquiries and first replies

The situation

Enquiries arrive by web form, email and LinkedIn. Someone checks the inbox when they can. Quotes are built by hand, and any discount or non-standard scope waits until the founder is free. Good leads go cold while the answer sits in a queue.

What is actually breaking

Not the speed of typing. Three things: nobody owns the first reply, the quote is rebuilt from scratch each time, and the discount limit is only in the founder's head, so every exception comes to them.

What I would fix before automating anything

Write down the discount limit and the scope the team can approve alone, and name who owns the first reply. Automating an undecided limit would just send the same exception to the founder faster.

The workflow I would build

  • New enquiry arrives
  • Enriched and scored
  • Reply and quote drafted
  • Sign-off above the limit
  • Project and onboarding start

Where a person approves

Anything within the written limits goes out under the account manager's name. Only a discount or scope above the limit reaches the founder's phone for a yes or no. The owner sets the limits and can change them at any time.

Scenario 2 of 4 · Onboarding

A 25-person professional services firm where onboarding is copy and paste

Single AI workflow sprint · client onboarding and handover

The situation

When a client signs, someone creates folders, sets up accounts in several tools, sends the welcome pack and books the kick-off. It takes the same steps every time, but it depends on one coordinator remembering them all. When that person is away, onboarding slips.

What is actually breaking

A repeated, rule-based process spread across four tools, with the steps held in one person's head and no written place where a non-standard client is handled.

What I would fix before automating anything

Write the steps as they really run, including what happens with a non-standard engagement. That written map is the product of the first 2-hour mapping session, and you keep it whether or not anything is built.

The workflow I would build

  • Client signs
  • Accounts and folders created
  • Welcome pack sent
  • Non-standard terms flagged
  • Kick-off booked

Where a person approves

Standard clients flow through with no one touching them. A client with non-standard terms, or a missing piece of information, is flagged to a named person before anything is sent to the client.

Scenario 3 of 4 · Cleanup

An 8-person firm with automations that half work and tools nobody remembers buying

AI cleanup sprint

The situation

Over the years someone set up a handful of Zaps and a couple of Make scenarios. Some fail silently, two do the same job, and the firm pays for overlapping subscriptions. Nobody can say what is still running or what it does.

What is actually breaking

No map. Without a list of what is live, who owns it and what it touches, fixing one thing breaks another, and nobody dares switch anything off.

What I would fix before automating anything

The first deliverable is the written map of every live automation. The fix follows the map, not the other way round.

The workflow I would build

  • Audit every live automation
  • Repair failing flows
  • Remove duplicate subscriptions
  • Owner approves what is switched off
  • Priority list handed over

Where a person approves

Nothing is switched off without the owner confirming it. Anything that touches client data or money is listed and approved before it is changed.

The sprint that fits

Failing flows repaired, duplicate tool subscriptions removed where I find them, a written map of every live automation and a priority list. 30 days of support follow.

Scenario 4 of 4 · End to end

A 40-person services firm where sales, delivery and finance all run through the owner

End-to-end AI ops engine

The situation

Enquiries, project handover, weekly reporting and invoicing each run in a different tool. The owner is the link between them: checking, approving, chasing and reconciling. A two-week absence is not something the business could survive.

What is actually breaking

The owner is the integration layer. Decisions, data and exceptions all route through one person because nothing connects the tools and nothing is written down about who can decide what.

What I would fix before automating anything

Decision rights first: who may approve what, up to which limit, and what must be escalated. At this size I would usually suggest starting with one pipeline before connecting all three.

The workflow I would build

  • Sales to delivery handover
  • Delivery status pulled together
  • Invoice raised on completion
  • Exceptions reach the owner
  • Weekly exceptions report

Where a person approves

The automation enforces the written limits, so routine work moves without the owner. The owner receives an exceptions report and approves only what is above their limits.
