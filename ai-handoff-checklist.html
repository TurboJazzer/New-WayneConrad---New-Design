Before You Hand a Process to AI: Five Questions

By Wayne Conrad  ·  October 15, 2026  ·  3 min read, print it

Short answer: before a process goes to AI, write down what good looks like, who checks it, who fixes it, the first step that cannot be undone, and who owns a client's questions about it. If you cannot put a name next to the checker, the process is not ready to start.

This is for owners of 5 to 50 person service firms. It takes about ten minutes per process. Do it with the person who does the job today, not for them.

1

What does good look like?

Write it in three lines. If the standard lives in one person's head, an agent inherits the guesswork, and so does the next hire.

2

Who reads it before it leaves?

One name, not "the team". "The team will check it" means nobody does. In a small firm the person who checks is often the busiest, so decide it before the agent starts.

3

Who fixes it when it is wrong?

A second name. Fixing a mistake is a different job from spotting one, and it needs someone with the time and authority to do it.

4

What is the first step that cannot be undone?

In a service firm it is usually sending to a client, paying a supplier or deleting a record. Everything before that step is draft work an agent can do alone. That step, and everything after it, gets a named reader.

Ask the person who does the job where that step really is. Owners and staff often disagree. A quote can look like a draft to the owner while the client has already read it.

5

Who answers when a client forwards an AI answer?

Name one person per client account. Their reply can be one line: yes because, or no because. Then tell the client who that person is, so they hear one view every time.

If you cannot fill in the name next to a process, it is not ready to hand over.

Earn a lighter check from a record

Do not set the trust level from a vendor's demo. Have the named reader read the first twenty or so outputs and keep a tally of how many needed a change. If none did, the task can move to a lighter check. If one did, it goes back, and the fix goes into the agent's instructions.
